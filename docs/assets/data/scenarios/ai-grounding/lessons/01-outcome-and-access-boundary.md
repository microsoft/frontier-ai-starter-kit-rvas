# Lesson 1 — Outcome and access boundary

## Goal

Define one bounded work decision and the evidence/access boundary that makes a grounded response safe.

## Duration

35 minutes.

## Audience

Business owner, frontline representative, source owner, security or privacy reviewer, and facilitator.

## Preparation

Bring one representative work request, a named source owner, and the fictional corpus map in `../accelerator/sample-data/README.md`. Do not use customer records in the workshop.

## Timed activity

| Time | Facilitation step |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Ask each participant to name one decision or action that a better answer would change. |
| 5–15 min | Draft: “When *role* receives *trigger*, it can *action* using *authoritative evidence*.” |
| 15–25 min | Map allowed roles, prohibited content, source owners, sensitivity, geography, and escalation. |
| 25–35 min | Challenge the boundary with an access-denied request and record the refusal/handoff. |

## Artifact

An outcome-and-access-boundary canvas with outcome, non-goals, authorized groups, source of record, prohibited content, and escalation owner.

## Expected output

For the Northstar fixture: a returns coordinator may explain policy and route exceptions; it may not approve supervisor exceptions, disclose account notes, payment details, or another customer’s history.

## Validation

Every proposed source has an owner and access group; every prohibited request has a refusal or escalation path. The team can identify the record that remains authoritative.

## Debrief

Ask: “What response would be useful but unsafe?” Distinguish an answer that is unavailable because of access from one that is unavailable because the record does not exist.

## Next decision

Choose the smallest decision journey to map in Lesson 2; defer sources that do not affect that decision.
